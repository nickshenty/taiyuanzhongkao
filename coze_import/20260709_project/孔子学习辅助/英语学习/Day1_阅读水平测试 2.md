## 文章1：The New Art Club

### The New Art Club

Last week, our school started a new art club. Miss Wang, our art teacher, is the leader of the club. She loves painting and wants to help students find their interest in art.

Every Tuesday after school, about twenty students come to Room 305 for the club. They bring their own pencils, brushes, and paper. Miss Wang shows them how to draw simple things first, like flowers and animals. Then she teaches them how to use watercolors.

Li Ming is a member of the club. He is fourteen years old. Before he joined the club, he thought art was boring. But now, he loves it. "I drew a picture of my cat yesterday," he said with a big smile. "It was not perfect, but I was really happy."

The club also has a small exhibition every month. Students put their best work on the wall of the hallway. Other students and teachers can see the pictures when they walk by. Miss Wang always picks three winners and gives them small gifts, like notebooks and color pencils.

Next month, the club will go to a museum in the city center. Miss Wang says they can see many famous paintings there. The students are very excited about the trip. "I can't wait to see real paintings by great artists," said Li Ming. "Maybe one day I can be a great artist too."

## 文章2：A Town That Changed Itself

### A Town That Changed Itself

Fifteen years ago, Greenfield was a dirty town. The river that ran through the town was polluted by factory waste. The air smelled bad, and few people wanted to walk outside. Many families had moved to other cities because the environment was so bad.

Then something changed. A group of high school students started a campaign called "Clean Greenfield." They wrote letters to the town government and asked the factories to stop polluting the river. At first, nobody took them seriously. But the students didn't give up. They organized clean-up events along the riverbank. They planted trees in empty areas. They even made short videos and posted them online to spread their message.

Their efforts have been noticed. Within three years, the government passed new rules to protect the river. The factories were required to clean their waste before releasing it. A new recycling center was built in the town. Today, the river is clean enough for fish to live in again. Birds have returned to the trees. Children play in the park by the river.

Maria, now a college student, was one of the students who started the campaign. "We were just teenagers," she said. "We didn't have money or power. But we believed that small actions could make a big difference. And we were right."

The story of Greenfield has been reported by several newspapers. Many other towns have followed its example. As Maria often says, "The Earth doesn't need a few perfect people. It needs millions of imperfect people doing their best."

## 文章3：The Double-Edged Sword of Social Media

### The Double-Edged Sword of Social Media

Social media has become an inseparable part of modern life, especially for teenagers. Platforms like WeChat, Instagram, and TikTok connect people across the globe, allowing them to share moments, exchange ideas, and maintain friendships that distance would have ended in the past. However, psychologists who study teenage behavior have noticed a worrying trend: the more time young people spend on social media, the more likely they are to feel anxious and lonely.

Dr. Sarah Chen, a researcher at a university in Beijing, has been studying this phenomenon for five years. She explains that social media creates an illusion of connection while actually reducing real, face-to-face interaction. "When you scroll through your friends' posts, you feel like you're connecting with them," she says. "But in reality, you are alone in your room, looking at a screen."

One of the biggest problems is what experts call "social comparison." Teenagers often compare their real lives with the carefully curated posts of their peers, which show only the best moments. A girl might see her classmate's photos from a beautiful beach vacation and feel that her own life is boring by comparison. She doesn't realize that those photos were selected from hundreds of others, and that her classmate also has ordinary, uneventful days.

Another concern is that social media can be addictive. The algorithms that these platforms use are designed to keep users engaged for as long as possible. Every "like" and comment releases a small amount of dopamine in the brain, which makes users want to check their phones again and again. Some teenagers check their phones over a hundred times a day.

However, Dr. Chen does not believe that the solution is to ban social media entirely. Instead, she suggests that young people should be taught how to use it wisely. "Social media is a tool," she says. "It's neither good nor bad by itself. What matters is how we choose to use it."

**3. What is "social comparison" as described in the passage?**

**4. Why is social media addictive, according to the passage?**

D. Because the government encourages it.

**5. What is Dr. Chen's final suggestion?**
A. Social media should be completely banned.
B. Social media should be replaced by other tools.

## 文章4：Lost in Translation

### Lost in Translation: Lessons from a Summer Abroad

If I had been told a year ago that a single summer in Spain would fundamentally change the way I view the world, I would have laughed. I had always considered myself open-minded, but nothing could have prepared me for the experience of living in a culture so different from my own.

I arrived in Seville, a small city in southern Spain, on a scorching July afternoon. I had been studying Spanish for two years, so I assumed I would communicate without difficulty. I was mistaken. The locals spoke with an accent I had never encountered, dropped syllables casually, and used expressions that no textbook had ever mentioned. When my host mother greeted me with "¿Qué tal?" and a kiss on each cheek, I froze. In China, we shake hands or nod politely. Kissing strangers was something I had only seen in movies.

That first evening at the dinner table was perhaps the most uncomfortable moment of my entire stay. Dinner began at nine-thirty — three hours later than I was accustomed to — and it lasted until midnight. The family talked over each other, laughed loudly, and gestured wildly with their hands. I sat in silence, smiling nervously, wishing I were back home where meals were quiet and orderly.

Over the following weeks, however, something remarkable happened. Had I not been forced to adapt, I might never have discovered how enriching discomfort can be. I learned to navigate the narrow, winding streets of the old city. I learned to appreciate the siesta, which initially seemed lazy to me but which I came to regard as a wise rhythm of life. Most importantly, I learned that what I had once considered "strange" was simply "different" — and that difference, far from being threatening, was the very thing that made the experience so transformative.

When I returned to China, I found myself looking at my own culture through new eyes. The customs I had taken for granted — removing shoes before entering a home, addressing elders with formal titles, the emphasis on collective harmony — suddenly seemed no more "natural" or "correct" than the Spanish customs I had initially found so jarring. If I could go back and do it again, I would change nothing.

## 文章5：The Ethical Dilemma of Artificial Intelligence

### The Ethical Dilemma of Artificial Intelligence

As artificial intelligence continues to permeate virtually every sphere of human activity — from healthcare diagnostics to autonomous transportation, from financial forecasting to judicial sentencing — the question of whether we are adequately prepared to govern this transformative technology has become one of the most pressing ethical challenges of our time. The paradox lies in the fact that while AI systems possess the capacity to process information and make decisions at speeds and scales far beyond human capability, they remain fundamentally dependent on the data upon which they are trained, data that inevitably reflects the biases, prejudices, and blind spots of the societies from which it is harvested.

Consider, for instance, the growing deployment of algorithmic systems in criminal justice. In several jurisdictions, courts have begun utilizing AI-driven risk assessment tools to determine whether defendants should be granted bail prior to trial. Proponents argue that such systems eliminate the subjective biases that human judges may harbor, thereby rendering the judicial process more equitable and consistent. Critics, however, contend that the opposite is true: because these algorithms are trained on historical data that itself reflects decades of discriminatory policing and sentencing practices, they tend to reproduce and even amplify the very inequities they are purported to resolve. A study conducted by researchers at a prominent university revealed that one widely used risk assessment tool was nearly twice as likely to falsely flag Black defendants as future criminals compared to their white counterparts — a disparity that persisted even when the algorithm was fed ostensibly neutral variables such as prior arrest records and age.

The implications extend well beyond the courtroom. In the domain of healthcare, AI-driven diagnostic systems have demonstrated remarkable accuracy in detecting certain types of cancer from medical imagery, yet studies have shown that these systems perform significantly less accurately when analyzing images of patients with darker skin tones, owing to the underrepresentation of such individuals in the training datasets. The consequences of such disparities are not merely theoretical; they manifest in delayed diagnoses, inadequate treatment plans, and, ultimately, in lives lost.

What renders this dilemma particularly intractable is the inherent tension between transparency and proprietary interest. The companies that develop these algorithms regard their underlying architectures as closely guarded trade secrets, which precludes independent researchers from subjecting them to rigorous external scrutiny. Consequently, when an algorithm produces a biased outcome, identifying the precise mechanism by which the bias operates — let alone rectifying it — becomes extraordinarily difficult, if not impossible.

The path forward is neither straightforward nor universally agreed upon. Some ethicists advocate for the establishment of independent regulatory bodies empowered to audit algorithmic systems before their deployment in high-stakes domains. Others argue that the very notion of "debiasing" an algorithm is fundamentally misguided, as any system trained on data produced by an imperfect society will inevitably encode that society's imperfections. What is abundantly clear, however, is that the question is no longer whether AI will reshape the institutions upon which we rely, but rather whether we possess the collective wisdom to ensure that it does so in a manner that serves the interests of all, rather than merely those who happen to design it.

### 文章3答案

**1. B**　文中写到 "the more time young people spend on social media, the more likely they are to feel anxious and lonely."

**2. B**　Dr. Chen 解释说社交媒体制造了连接的错觉，"you feel like you're connecting with them... But in reality, you are alone in your room, looking at a screen."

**3. B**　文中描述 "social comparison" 是 "Teenagers often compare their real lives with the carefully curated posts of their peers."

**4. B**　文中写到 "Every 'like' and comment releases a small amount of dopamine in the brain, which makes users want to check their phones again and again."

**5. C**　Dr. Chen 最后建议 "young people should be taught how to use it wisely."

**重点词汇：**

| 英文 | 中文释义 |
|------|----------|
| inseparable | adj. 不可分离的 |
| illusion | n. 错觉；幻觉 |
| curate | v. 精选；策划 |
| addictive | adj. 使人上瘾的 |
| dopamine | n. 多巴胺 |

**全文翻译：**

社交媒体已经成为现代生活中不可分割的一部分，尤其对青少年而言。微信、Instagram和TikTok等平台将全球各地的人连接在一起，让他们分享瞬间、交流想法、维持那些在过去会因距离而中断的友谊。然而，研究青少年行为的心理学家注意到了一个令人担忧的趋势：年轻人在社交媒体上花的时间越多，就越容易感到焦虑和孤独。

陈莎拉博士是北京一所大学的研究员，已经研究这一现象五年了。她解释说，社交媒体制造了一种连接的错觉，实际上却减少了真正面对面的交流。"当你浏览朋友的动态时，你觉得自己在和他们交流，"她说，"但实际上，你是一个人待在房间里，盯着屏幕。"

最大的问题之一是专家所说的"社交比较"。青少年经常将自己的真实生活与同龄人精心筛选过的动态进行对比，而那些动态只展示了最好的瞬间。一个女孩可能看到同学在海滨度假的照片，就觉得自己平淡的生活很无聊。她没有意识到，那些照片是从数百张中挑选出来的，而她的同学同样有许多平凡的日子。

另一个令人担忧的是社交媒体可能让人上瘾。这些平台使用的算法旨在让用户尽可能长时间地保持活跃。每一次"点赞"和评论都会在大脑中释放少量的多巴胺，让用户想要一次又一次地查看手机。有些青少年每天查看手机超过一百次。

然而，陈博士并不认为解决方案是完全禁止社交媒体。相反，她建议应该教会年轻人如何明智地使用它。"社交媒体是一种工具，"她说，"它本身既不好也不坏。重要的是我们选择如何使用它。"

### 文章4答案

我在一个炎热的七月下午抵达了塞维利亚，西班牙南部的一座小城。我已经学了两年西班牙语，所以我以为沟通不会有困难。我错了。当地人说话带着我从未遇到过的口音，随意地省略音节，还使用着教科书上从未提到过的表达方式。当我的寄宿妈妈用"¿Qué tal?"（你好吗？）问候我并在我两边脸颊各亲了一下时，我僵住了。在中国，我们握手或礼貌地点头。亲吻陌生人这种事我只在电影里见过。

第一个晚上在餐桌上的经历，也许是我整个住宿期间最不自在的时刻。晚餐九点半才开始——比我的习惯晚了三个小时——而且一直持续到午夜。这家人说话时互相抢话，大声笑，手势挥舞得非常夸张。我默默地坐着，紧张地微笑着，真希望回到那个吃饭安静有序的家里。

**全文翻译：**

随着人工智能继续渗透到人类活动的几乎每一个领域——从医疗诊断到自动驾驶，从金融预测到司法量刑——我们是否已做好充分准备来治理这项变革性技术，已成为当今最紧迫的伦理挑战之一。矛盾在于，虽然AI系统拥有以远超人类能力的速度和规模处理信息并做出决策的能力，但它们从根本上仍然依赖于训练所用的数据，而这些数据不可避免地反映了其所来源社会中的偏见、歧视和盲点。

以刑事司法领域为例，算法系统的部署日益增多。在多个司法管辖区，法院已开始使用AI驱动的风险评估工具来决定被告在审判前是否应获得保释。支持者认为，这些系统消除了人类法官可能怀有的主观偏见，从而使司法过程更加公平和一致。然而，批评者认为事实恰恰相反：因为这些算法是在历史数据上训练的，而历史数据本身就反映了数十年歧视性的执法和量刑实践，所以它们倾向于复制甚至放大它们声称要解决的不平等。一项由一所知名大学的研究人员进行的研究揭示，一种广泛使用的风险评估工具误判黑人被告为未来罪犯的可能性几乎是白人被告的两倍——即使算法被输入了表面上中性的变量，如前科记录和年龄，这种差距仍然存在。

其影响远不限于法庭。在医疗领域，AI驱动的诊断系统在从医学影像中检测某些类型的癌症方面表现出了惊人的准确性，但研究表明，在分析肤色较深患者的影像时，这些系统的准确性明显降低，原因是此类个体在训练数据集中代表性不足。这种差距的后果绝不仅仅是理论上的；它们表现为延误诊断、不充分的治疗方案，最终表现为生命的丧失。

使这一困境特别棘手的是透明度与商业利益之间的内在矛盾。开发这些算法的公司将底层架构视为严密守护的商业机密，这使得独立研究人员无法对其进行严格的外部审查。因此，当算法产生有偏见的结果时，要确定偏见运作的精确机制——更不用说纠正它——变得极其困难，甚至不可能。

前进的道路既不简单，也没有普遍共识。一些伦理学家主张建立独立的监管机构，被授权在算法系统部署到高风险领域之前对其进行审计。另一些人则认为，"消除算法偏见"这一概念本身就是根本性错误的，因为任何在不完美社会产生的数据上训练的系统，都将不可避免地编码该社会的不完美。然而，有一件事是清楚的：问题不再是AI是否会重塑我们所依赖的机构，而是我们是否拥有集体智慧，确保它以服务于所有人利益的方式这样做，而不仅仅是那些恰好设计它的人的利益。
